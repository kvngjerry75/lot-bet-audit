# Independent audit of astro-latest: October 2026

Auditor's run: 26 Oct 2026, on the data in the bundle (Afriluck 29 Jun 2024 to 26 Oct 2026 Anopa, 2,061 draws;
DayWA 26 Nov 2019 to 24 Oct 2026, 2,076 draws). Every number below comes from code in this bundle; the commands that
reproduce them are in section 10.

## 1. Verdict in plain words

**The system is honest and correct. It does not make money, and no change I could make does either.**

- The engineering holds up. I found **no leakage**: I scrambled the last 200–300 draws and checked every one of the
  16 forecasts, on both games. Not a single forecast for an earlier draw moved. The pooled Afriluck timeline, the
  dedup and your payout maths are all correct. I found four small code bugs and one bad data row, all fixed; none of
  them changed a backtest result.
- The pink bias is real (pink exit strength ×1.112, 95% interval 1.073–1.152, z ≈ 6). It is steady over time, the
  same in every session, and the models do capture it. Sharp's Perm-2 return beats 200 of 200 fair machines
  (p = 0.005).
- **The payout is bigger than the edge, by a wide margin.** Suppose you knew the pink bias *exactly*, with no
  forecasting error at all. The best Afriluck bet would still return about **62p per GH₵1** (at most 65p at the top
  of the bias's 95% interval). Every bet type is below break-even: Perm-2 62p, Banker 59p, Direct-1 55p,
  Direct-3 48p, Direct-4 47p. For Perm-2 to break even, the pink balls would have to be about **twice** as strong
  as they are.
- I looked hard for an edge beyond colour: pairs that come out together, ball-set changes, the machine numbers and
  the exit order. **I found three new real effects, and none of them can be bet.** The most interesting one: the 6
  machine numbers are *not* the next six balls of the same random process (z = −8). The 12 balls taken together are
  close to fair; the pink ones simply tend to come out first. Section 4 has the details.
- Of 62 forecaster × bet combinations replayed walk-forward in GH₵, **none beats break-even after correction for
  multiple testing.** The few rows above 100% rest on one to three lucky hits.
- DayWA has no edge that survives fair-machine controls and correction for the 16 models tried. Its prize table is
  not published anyway.
- **Stake sizing:** Kelly says stake **GH₵0** on every bet. If you play for fun, the smallest-loss way is in section 8.

The changes are small but real: the MAIN BEAST no longer dilutes sharp, stage 4 can now see the pink bias, and there
are three new tools. `bet_lab.py` replays every bet in GH₵, `dependence.py` searches for an edge beyond colour, and
`forward_test.py` logs picks before each draw with a fixed stop rule. **They do not turn a loss into a profit**, and
I will not tell you otherwise.

## 4. The improvement attempts (your list B1–B9)

1. **Optimise for the bet.** The dependence tests found no pair effect, and the pink bias is a Plackett–Luce
   per-ball effect, so the joint pair model ranks pairs exactly like the product of the ball probabilities. The best
   core is simply the top pink balls. Core size changes the stake, not the return per GH₵1. Measured (draws 561–2060,
   new BEAST): top 3 72.0p, top 4 70.0p, top 5 67.2p, top 6 62.4p, top 8 63.3p, top 10 64.4p. Every interval
   overlaps every other, and all are below 100p.
2. **Dependence between balls.** None of the clustering kind. Draws are, if anything, more spread out than random
   (F15, F16).
3. **Ball-set changes.** None (F17).
4. **Fix the combiner.** Done (F10, F12). Scored on the untouched half (1311–2060), the new BEAST minus the old one
   is +1.8p per GH₵1 on Perm-2 top 10 (95% CI −5.7 to +9.4). It removes a dilution; it does not create an edge.
5. **Stage 4 with long-run frequency.** Done (F11). It helps stage 4's MLP and ranker, but not past sharp. The TFT
   army must be checked in Colab (section 10).
6. **Machine numbers and exit order.** New physics found (F13, F14). Using it for winning numbers adds nothing
   (F14). Direct-1 on the top ball: 64.7p walk-forward (95% CI 45.9–88.5p) against a ceiling of 55–56p, so that
   figure is luck on 1,761 draws, not an edge.

## 6. Physical data: what to measure and what to ask for

What would have to be true for a profit: a pair lift of ×1.77, roughly two balls each about ×1.33 more likely to be
drawn than now. Such a ball would appear about 290 times in 2,061 draws instead of about 217. The most frequent ball
now (16) appears 258 times, consistent with pink plus noise. So any physical cause would have to be far stronger than
anything visible. Physical data is more likely to *explain* the pink bias than to beat the payout. It is still worth
collecting, because it is the only route left.

**From the live-stream videos, per draw:**
1. The **full exit order of all 12 balls** (6 winning, then 6 machine), with the second each ball exits. This tests
   F13–F14 directly and gives the true machine order (F5).
2. **Rack position before release**: which slot each numbered ball sits in, if the rack is visible. A loading-order
   effect is the most likely explanation of "pink first" and of the per-ball early tendency.
3. **Mixing time**: seconds from release to the first exit. Shorter mixing should mean a stronger order bias, which
   would also mean a stronger pink edge on those draws.
4. Whether the balls are **re-racked or re-mixed** between the winning and the machine numbers.
5. Any **ball-set label** or a visibly new or worn ball.

**Ask the operator (Afriluck, and the NLA as regulator) for:**
- the official **prize table** for every bet type (it decides everything: see section 9);
- **ball-set replacement dates** and the set in use for each draw;
- **ball mass and diameter** for each numbered ball, and the tolerance;
- the **machine model**, the **rack loading procedure** (fixed slot per number? which colour is loaded first?) and the
  mixing time.

**How I would use it:** rack position or mixing time becomes an extra input to sharp (ball rate = colour × position
effect), tested walk-forward against sharp with the same rules as here. A replacement date becomes a split point for
a model fitted only to the current set. Weights become a per-ball prior. Each would go through `bet_lab.py` and
`forward_test.py` before any stake.

## 8. Stake sizing

Kelly stake = (expected return − 1) / (spread of returns). With every expected return below 1, **Kelly stakes GH₵0**
on every bet, and so does any fraction of Kelly. There is no bankroll plan that turns a 62p-per-GH₵1 bet into a profit.

If you play anyway for entertainment, the smallest-loss way is a **fixed monthly budget, Perm-2 on the top 3 pink
numbers**: 3 pairs, GH₵3 a draw. It has the same 62p return per GH₵1 as bigger cores, but risks the least per draw.
Simulated at the measured pink bias:

| Plan for one month | Staked | Expected loss | Chance of being ahead | Chance of losing more than half |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Top 3, one draw a day (30 draws) | GH₵90 | GH₵34 | 23% | 41% |
| Top 3, every draw (90) | GH₵270 | GH₵101 | 16% | 48% |
| Top 10, one draw a day (30) | GH₵1,350 | GH₵508 | 6.5% | 34% |
| Top 10, every draw (90) | GH₵4,050 | GH₵1,517 | 0.5% | 16% |

## 9. What would change the verdict

- **A better payout.** At the measured bias, Perm-2 on pink numbers breaks even at **97× per pair** or more (now
  60×); Direct-1 at about 55× (now 30×); Banker at about 101× per pair; Direct-3 at about 1,250×. A promotion or cashback that
  lifts the effective payout this far would make a bet worth testing. Ask for the official table first.
- **A ball far out of line**, from damage or replacement: a single ball near ×1.33. `dependence.py` and `monitor.py`
  would flag it within a few hundred draws.
- **Video data** showing a rack-position or mixing-time effect much stronger than the colour effect (section 6).

## 10. Forward-test plan

Fixed in advance, in `forward_test.py`. The null is that a bet returns what the pink bias allows (0.62 per GH₵1); the
claim is that it returns at least 1.00. The test uses a 1% one-sided false-alarm rate and 90% power. Log every pick
**before** its draw; late picks are never counted.

| Bet | GH₵ per draw | Draws needed | About |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perm-2 top 10 | 45 | 141 | 7 weeks at 20 draws a week |
| Perm-2 top 6 | 15 | 332 | 17 weeks |
| Banker | 56 | 255 | 13 weeks |
| Perm-2 top 3 | 3 | 1,279 | 64 weeks |
| Direct-1 | 1 | 1,480 | 74 weeks |

- **Stop rule:** after 30+ logged draws, stop as soon as the upper 99% bound of the running return falls below 1.00.
  A break-even edge is then ruled out.
- **Confirm only** after the planned number of draws, if the return is ≥ 1.00 **and** its lower 99% bound is above
  0.62. Only then would a stake (fractional Kelly from `bet_lab.py`) be justified.
- Proving even the *existing* pink edge (0.62 against 0.564 for luck) on future draws alone would take about 3,000
  draws. That is why it took the whole history.
